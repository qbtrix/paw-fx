A mascot with twenty-eight states.

One drawing, twenty-eight poses that morph into each other, and a gaze that follows your pointer. The pose is a pure function of time, so it pauses, scrubs and exports frame-exact — and the character is an input, so the whole thing works just as well on your drawing as on this one.

Poke it. Hover it, click it, or fling your pointer past it. Those are layered on whatever it is doing rather than replacing it: prodding a mascot that is thinking makes it flinch and carry on thinking.

Every state

Each one is live, not a screenshot, and only the ones on your screen are drawing. Click any of them to drive the one above. The last twelve are the anime shorthand: a sweat bead, a vein pop, spirals, steam, tears, flame. They lean on marks, colour and a mouth, because eye shape was most of what the first sixteen had and it had run out.

Or drive it by mood

The states are authored, which is why they read well — and what they cannot do is sit between two of themselves. An agent's condition is not one of a list of labels; it is confidence 0.2 and load 0.8. Three axes, and any point on them is a face.

A point cannot reach everything, and it is worth saying plainly: wink
  and smug are asymmetries, happy, love, dizzy and
  starstruck replace the eyes with a drawn face, and the anime shorthand runs on marks,
  tints and a mouth rather than on eye shape. Those are characters, not coordinates. The mood space
  gives you the capsule-eyed range; the rest stay presets.






  
Bring your own mascot

  
The engine does not know what a Paw is. It is handed three silhouettes, two pivots
  and an eye box; everything after is the same whatever the character. Drop an SVG that follows the
  contract and it runs the identical derivation the command line does, in this page, on your file.
  Nothing is uploaded — the file never leaves your browser.

    The contract
    
  
    

    <path   id="paw-head"   d="…"/>   the skull, one closed M/C path
<path   id="paw-ear-l"  d="…"/>   left ear, starting at the point it hangs from
<path   id="paw-ear-r"  d="…"/>   right ear, same
<rect   id="paw-eye-l" …/>        the resting eye (or an <ellipse>)
<circle id="paw-catch" …/>        optional, a glass catch inside that eye
<g      id="paw-ground">…</g>     optional, behind everything, never clipped
<g      id="paw-sheen">…</g>      optional, clipped to the head
<defs>…</defs>                    the head's own fill and stroke become the
                                  material, so name your gradients freely
    
  
    
  
    Where an ear hangs from is the one thing that cannot be derived — it is a
  fact about the character, not about the outline — so its path has to start there. Everything
  else comes off the drawing: scale and centre from the head's box, eye size and separation from the
  eye, the material from the defs.
    
  
    One limit, and it is reported rather than hidden. Each part becomes
  r(θ) about a single origin, which is what buys the morphing. Every ray has to leave
  the outline exactly once. Domes, blobs and lobe ears are fine; a tail, an antenna or a notch deep
  enough for a ray to cross twice gets flattened, and the check above says so.
